## Overview

Augustus concentrates power while preserving the language and many offices of the Republic. The title princeps emphasizes his position as leading citizen. Military loyalty, provincial authority, and tribunician power support the new arrangement.

His reign changes Rome's administration and appearance. Agrippa's public works, fire protection, grain supply, and succession plans provide useful clues. Balance domestic achievements with the limits of expansion, especially Varus' defeat in Germany.

## Key connections

- Octavian received the title **Augustus** in 27 BCE. This date conventionally begins the imperial period.

- Augustus' political system is called the **Principate**. It takes its name from princeps, the leading citizen.

- The commander who lost three legions in Germany in 9 CE was **Varus**. His full name was Publius Quinctilius Varus.

- The German leader who defeated Varus was **Arminius**. He belonged to the Cherusci.

- Augustus' Forum contained the Temple of Mars **Ultor**. Ultor means avenger.

- Augustus' successor in 14 CE was **Tiberius**. He was Livia's son and had been adopted by Augustus.
