## Overview

Nerva, Trajan, Hadrian, Antoninus Pius, and Marcus Aurelius form the traditional Five Good Emperors. Adoption is a recurring succession mechanism. Trajan expands the Empire; Hadrian emphasizes consolidation and provincial travel.

Marcus Aurelius shares power with Lucius Verus and faces northern frontier wars. His biological son Commodus succeeds him. Commodus' death is followed by the brief reigns of Pertinax and Didius Julianus and a new struggle for power.

## Key connections

- The emperor adopted by Nerva was **Trajan**. The Empire reached its greatest territorial extent under him.

- Trajan defeated the Dacian king **Decebalus**. Trajan's Column commemorates the Dacian wars.

- The emperor who rebuilt the Pantheon and built a wall in Britain was **Hadrian**. Antoninus Pius built a different wall farther north.

- The emperor between Hadrian and Marcus Aurelius was Antoninus **Pius**. He ruled from 138 to 161 CE.

- The emperor who wrote the Meditations was **Marcus Aurelius**. He was a Stoic and co-ruled with Lucius Verus until 169 CE.

- Marcus Aurelius' son who succeeded him was **Commodus**. He cultivated an identification with Hercules.
