## Overview

The Social War concerns the Italian allies' demand for citizenship. Laws extending citizenship help resolve it, but struggles over military command soon lead to civil war. Sulla first marches on Rome after the Mithridatic command is transferred to Marius.

Sulla fights in Greece, returns to Italy, and secures power through victory and dictatorship. His constitutional changes strengthen the Senate and restrict tribunes; his proscriptions target political opponents. The wars with Mithridates continue beyond Sulla, eventually bringing Lucullus and Pompey to the center of eastern affairs.

## Key connections

- The Social War concerned Roman **citizenship** for the Italian allies. It is also called the Italian or Marsic War.

- The king of Pontus who fought three wars with Rome was Mithridates **VI**. Keep him distinct from his son Pharnaces II.

- The commander who first marched on Rome in 88 BCE was **Sulla**. The dispute concerned the Mithridatic command.

- Sulla's peace of 85 BCE was the Treaty of **Dardanus**. It ended the First Mithridatic War.

- Sulla secured control of Rome at the **Colline** Gate in 82 BCE. Crassus commanded his right wing.

- The law transferring the Mithridatic command to Pompey was the Lex **Manilia**. The guide dates it to 66 BCE; the Lex Gabinia concerned pirates.
