## Overview

Harrison begins with supporting lists because monuments and geography connect many periods. Learn a structure with its builder, function, and location. A road or aqueduct may identify a statesman before a question reaches his best-known achievement.

Use this lesson to connect the course's periods. Review the emperor sequence in the original guide alongside these lessons. For advanced preparation, revisit the full notes for additional commanders, laws, family members, and short reigns. These lessons prioritize a usable foundation across the guide's timeline.

## Key connections

- The censor who built the Via Appia and Aqua Appia was Appius Claudius **Caecus**. Both projects date to his censorship in 312 BCE.

- The Via Appia originally connected Rome with **Capua**. It supported Rome's connections with Campania.

- The road associated with Gaius Flaminius was the Via **Flaminia**. It ran from Rome to Ariminum.

- The builder associated with the Aqua Virgo was **Agrippa**. He also built the original Pantheon.

- Trajan's architect was Apollodorus of **Damascus**. The guide associates him with Trajan's Forum and bridge.

- The eastern emperor associated with the Corpus Iuris Civilis was **Justinian**. His reign continued the Roman legal tradition after 476.
