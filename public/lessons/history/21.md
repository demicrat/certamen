## Overview

Diocletian addresses succession and defense by sharing power. The Tetrarchy has two senior Augusti and two junior Caesars. It is intended as a coordinated system of government and succession.

The system breaks down after the first abdications. Constantine emerges from civil wars, defeating Maxentius in the west and Licinius in the east. His reign changes Christianity's legal position and establishes Constantinople as a major imperial center.

## Key connections

- Diocletian's four-ruler system was the **Tetrarchy**. It consisted of two Augusti and two Caesars.

- Diocletian's fellow Augustus in the west was **Maximian**. Galerius and Constantius Chlorus were the junior Caesars.

- Diocletian and Maximian abdicated in **305** CE. Their succession arrangements soon gave way to conflict.

- Constantine defeated Maxentius at the **Milvian** Bridge in 312 CE. This gave Constantine control of the west.

- Constantine defeated **Licinius** in 324 CE to gain sole rule. The final victory was at Chrysopolis.

- The former Byzantium was dedicated as **Constantinople** in 330 CE. The city became a major imperial capital.
