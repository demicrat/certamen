## Overview

Expansion intensifies disputes over land, citizenship, and authority. Tiberius and Gaius Gracchus pursue reforms through the tribunate. Their violent deaths are turning points in the political narrative of the late Republic.

Marius rises as a successful commander outside the old consular aristocracy. His career connects the Jugurthine War with the wars against the Cimbri and Teutones. Sulla becomes prominent in the same Numidian conflict, introducing the rivalry that dominates the next lesson.

## Key connections

- The mother of the Gracchi was **Cornelia**. She was Scipio Africanus' daughter.

- Tiberius Gracchus served as tribune and died in **133** BCE. His program centered on redistribution of public land.

- Gaius Gracchus first became tribune in **123** BCE. He served again in 122 BCE and died in 121 BCE.

- The general from Arpinum who held seven consulships was **Marius**. Gaius Marius was a novus homo.

- The Numidian king captured after Bocchus betrayed him was **Jugurtha**. Sulla negotiated the surrender during Marius' command.

- Marius defeated the Teutones at **Aquae Sextiae** in 102 BCE. He and Catulus defeated the Cimbri at Vercellae the following year.
