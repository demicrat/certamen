## Overview

The next three kings offer useful contrasts. Numa represents religious organization and peace; Tullus represents warfare and the destruction of Alba Longa; Ancus combines religious tradition with expansion and construction. Learn their achievements as clusters.

The Horatii and Curiatii belong to Tullus' conflict with Alba Longa. Ancus' association with Ostia and the first bridge points toward Rome's growing use of the Tiber. These links between ruler, institution, and location help when a question begins with a less familiar clue.

## Key connections

- Rome's second king, associated with religious institutions, was **Numa Pompilius**. Numa was a Sabine and was linked to the nymph Egeria.

- The nymph who advised Numa was **Egeria**. Use her name as an identification clue for Numa.

- The king associated with destroying Alba Longa was **Tullus Hostilius**. The combat of the Horatii and Curiatii belongs to his reign.

- Tullus' senate house was the Curia **Hostilia**. The building name preserves the king's name.

- The king credited with founding Ostia was **Ancus Marcius**. He was Numa's grandson.

- Ancus' wooden bridge over the Tiber was the Pons **Sublicius**. Keep it distinct from later battle sites such as the Milvian Bridge.
