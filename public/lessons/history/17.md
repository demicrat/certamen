## Overview

The year of four emperors runs from Galba through Otho and Vitellius to Vespasian. The first battle at Bedriacum ends Otho's challenge; the second brings victory to Vespasian's side. Provincial armies decide the succession.

Vespasian begins the Flavian dynasty, followed by his sons Titus and Domitian. Associate the family with the Jewish War and Colosseum. Keep Titus' short reign and its disasters distinct from Domitian's longer reign. Agricola's British campaigns form a separate military thread.

## Key connections

- The emperors of 69 were Galba, Otho, Vitellius, and **Vespasian**. He founded the Flavian dynasty.

- Otho was defeated at the first battle of **Bedriacum**. It is also called the First Battle of Cremona.

- Vespasian's son who captured Jerusalem in 70 CE was **Titus**. He later ruled from 79 to 81 CE.

- The Flavian Amphitheater is commonly called the **Colosseum**. Vespasian began it and Titus completed it.

- The volcano that erupted in 79 CE was **Vesuvius**. Pompeii and Herculaneum were among the affected towns.

- Agricola defeated Calgacus at Mons **Graupius**. The battle belongs to the northern British campaigns under Domitian.
