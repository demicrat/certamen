## Overview

After Constantine, family divisions, civil wars, and new dynasties reshape imperial power. Julian attempts to restore pagan religious practice; later rulers face Gothic movements and major defeats. Theodosius I is the last emperor to rule both east and west before his sons succeed him.

The fifth-century western narrative is a gradual loss of territory and authority, punctuated by sacks and struggles between emperors and generals. Romulus Augustulus' deposition is a conventional endpoint. Julius Nepos retains a claim until 480, and the Eastern Empire continues.

## Key connections

- The emperor called the Apostate was **Julian**. He attempted to restore paganism and died campaigning against Persia.

- Valens was killed at **Adrianople** in 378 CE. The Goths were led by Fritigern.

- The last emperor to rule east and west was **Theodosius** I. After 395, Honorius ruled west and Arcadius east.

- The Visigoth who sacked Rome in 410 CE was **Alaric**. Honorius was the western emperor.

- The Vandal king who sacked Rome in 455 CE was **Gaiseric**. His forces had already captured Carthage.

- The ruler who deposed Romulus Augustulus in 476 CE was **Odoacer**. Zeno ruled in the east; Julius Nepos retained a western claim.
