## Overview

The foundation narrative connects Rome to Alba Longa through Numitor's family. Romulus and Remus survive an attempt to destroy them, grow up outside the royal household, and return to restore their grandfather. Their dispute over the new settlement ends with Romulus as its sole founder.

In Roman tradition, Romulus creates both a community and its first institutions. The conflict with the Sabines ends in a shared population and joint kingship. Keep the twins' childhood, the Sabine conflict, and Romulus' deification in separate groups so that similar names do not blur together.

## Key connections

- The mother of Romulus and Remus was **Rhea Silvia**. She was the daughter of Numitor, the displaced ruler of Alba Longa.

- The shepherd who raised the twins was **Faustulus**. The guide names Acca Larentia as his wife.

- Romulus chose the **Palatine** Hill for his settlement. Remus is associated with the Aventine.

- The Sabine king who shared power with Romulus was **Titus Tatius**. Joint rule followed the conflict over the abducted Sabine women.

- Romulus won the first spolia opima by killing **Acron**. The spoils were dedicated to Jupiter Feretrius.

- Romulus was identified after his disappearance with the god **Quirinus**. This is the divine identity of Rome's first king.
