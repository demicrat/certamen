## Overview

The First Punic War begins with competing interventions in Sicily and becomes a long struggle with Carthage. Rome develops naval power while relying on boarding and close combat. Remember naval success, a failed African invasion, further disaster, and eventual victory.

The settlement gives Rome Sicily, its first province. The years before the Second Punic War also include expansion against the Gauls and action against Illyrian piracy. The middle Republic's military history extends beyond Carthage alone.

## Key connections

- The First Punic War began in **264** BCE. It ended in 241 BCE.

- Duilius won Rome's first major naval victory in 260 BCE at **Mylae**. The Romans used boarding bridges called corvi.

- The Spartan mercenary who defeated Regulus in Africa was **Xanthippus**. The defeat occurred in the Bagradas Valley in 255 BCE.

- The naval victory that ended the First Punic War was at the **Aegates** Islands. Lutatius Catulus won in 241 BCE.

- Rome's first province was **Sicily**. It came under Roman control after the First Punic War.

- Marcellus won spolia opima at **Clastidium** in 222 BCE. He killed the Insubrian leader Virodamarus in single combat.
