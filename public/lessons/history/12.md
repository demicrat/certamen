## Overview

Crassus suppresses Spartacus' revolt, Pompey gains extraordinary commands, and Cicero confronts Catiline's conspiracy. These careers overlap with Caesar's rise and the increasing power of personal alliances.

The First Triumvirate is an informal alliance of Caesar, Pompey, and Crassus. Caesar's campaigns in Gaul give him an army and prestige; Julia's and Crassus' deaths weaken the alliance. Learn the Gallic campaigns separately from the later civil war: Vercingetorix is Caesar's Gallic opponent, while Pompey becomes his Roman rival.

## Key connections

- The leader of the slave revolt beginning at Capua in 73 BCE was **Spartacus**. Crassus suppressed it; Pompey claimed a share of the credit.

- The consul who exposed Catiline's conspiracy in 63 BCE was **Cicero**. His full name was Marcus Tullius Cicero.

- Caesar and Pompey formed the First Triumvirate with **Crassus**. The alliance dates to 60 BCE.

- Caesar's daughter who married Pompey was **Julia**. Her death in 54 BCE removed a family tie.

- Crassus died fighting the Parthians at **Carrhae** in 53 BCE. His death helped unravel the triumvirate.

- Caesar forced Vercingetorix to surrender at **Alesia** in 52 BCE. Vercingetorix had earlier defeated Caesar at Gergovia.
