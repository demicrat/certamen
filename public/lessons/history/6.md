## Overview

Roman expansion in Italy alternates between victories and severe defeats. Camillus is linked with the capture of Veii and with the tradition of recovery after the Gallic sack. The Samnite Wars move the focus toward central and southern Italy.

Learn the Caudine Forks as a Roman humiliation and Sentinum as a victory. Pyrrhus enters at Tarentum's request; his costly successes explain the expression Pyrrhic victory. Roman success at Beneventum comes near the end of this sequence, before the wars with Carthage.

## Key connections

- The Roman credited with capturing Veii in 396 BCE was **Camillus**. His full name was Marcus Furius Camillus.

- The Gallic leader associated with the Allia disaster and sack of Rome was **Brennus**. The guide uses the traditional date 390 BCE.

- The Samnite trap of 321 BCE occurred at the **Caudine** Forks. Gavius Pontius forced the defeated Romans under the yoke.

- The major Roman victory of 295 BCE was at **Sentinum**. Fabius Maximus Rullianus and Publius Decius Mus commanded the Romans.

- The king of Epirus brought to Italy by Tarentum was **Pyrrhus**. His victories at Heraclea and Asculum were costly.

- Curius Dentatus defeated Pyrrhus in 275 BCE at **Beneventum**. Tarentum surrendered in 272 BCE.
