## Overview

After Hannibal, Roman power extends farther into the Greek world, Asia, and Spain. Match Philip V with Flamininus and Perseus with Aemilius Paullus. The Seleucid conflict introduces Antiochus III and the settlement at Apamea.

The year 146 BCE is an anchor because both Carthage and Corinth are destroyed. Scipio Aemilianus later takes Numantia in Spain. Keep Aemilianus distinct from Scipio Africanus, the victor at Zama: their campaigns belong to different generations.

## Key connections

- Flamininus defeated Philip V at **Cynoscephalae** in 197 BCE. He proclaimed Greek freedom at the Isthmian Games the next year.

- The Treaty of **Apamea** ended the Seleucid War in 188 BCE. Rome had defeated Antiochus III at Magnesia.

- Aemilius Paullus defeated Perseus at **Pydna** in 168 BCE. This decided the Third Macedonian War.

- The commander who destroyed Carthage in 146 BCE was Scipio **Aemilianus**. He also captured Numantia in 133 BCE.

- The commander who destroyed Corinth was Lucius **Mummius**. Corinth and Carthage were both destroyed in 146 BCE.

- The king who left Pergamum to Rome in 133 BCE was Attalus **III**. The bequest became the basis of the province of Asia.
