## Overview

Caesar's heir Octavian enters politics amid the struggle after the assassination. Unlike the First Triumvirate, the Second Triumvirate has a legal foundation. Its members defeat Caesar's assassins, divide control, and turn against one another.

Agrippa is the naval commander to remember beside Octavian. Naulochus removes Sextus Pompey's threat to the grain supply; Actium defeats Antony and Cleopatra. Octavian's eventual victory provides the foundation for the imperial system.

## Key connections

- Caesar's adopted heir who became Augustus was **Octavian**. He was born Gaius Octavius.

- Octavian and Antony formed the Second Triumvirate with **Lepidus**. Marcus Aemilius Lepidus was the third member.

- The law legalizing the Second Triumvirate was the Lex **Titia**. It was enacted in 43 BCE.

- Brutus and Cassius were defeated at **Philippi** in 42 BCE. Octavian and Antony commanded the victorious side.

- Agrippa defeated Sextus Pompey at **Naulochus** in 36 BCE. The naval battle took place off Sicily.

- Octavian and Agrippa defeated Antony and Cleopatra at **Actium** in 31 BCE. The victory paved the way for Octavian's sole rule.
