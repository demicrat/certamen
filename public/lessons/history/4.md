## Overview

The final kings are Tarquinius Priscus, Servius Tullius, and Tarquinius Superbus. Their stories combine public construction, civic organization, and violent struggles for succession. Tanaquil links Priscus with Servius; Tullia links Servius' death with Superbus' accession.

The assault on Lucretia by Sextus Tarquinius leads to the uprising against the royal family in Roman tradition. Lucius Junius Brutus becomes a central figure in the Republic's foundation. Keep him distinct from Marcus Junius Brutus, who appears centuries later in Caesar's assassination.

## Key connections

- The wife of Tarquinius Priscus who supported Servius was **Tanaquil**. She interpreted signs associated with both men's rise.

- The king especially associated with the census was **Servius Tullius**. His reforms organized citizens by wealth and centuries.

- Rome's last king was Tarquinius **Superbus**. His name is commonly rendered Tarquin the Proud.

- The great sewer associated with the Tarquins was the Cloaca **Maxima**. The guide connects its completion with Superbus.

- The woman whose assault precipitated the revolt against the monarchy was **Lucretia**. Sextus Tarquinius was her attacker.

- The leader of the revolt against the Tarquins was Lucius Junius **Brutus**. He became one of the traditional first consuls.
