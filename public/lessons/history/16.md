## Overview

Follow the succession Tiberius, Caligula, Claudius, Nero. Family relationships help explain the order, but each reign has distinct clues. Sejanus belongs with Tiberius, Cassius Chaerea with Caligula's death, and the invasion of Britain with Claudius.

Nero's reign combines an influential early advisory circle with later conspiracies and revolt. The fire of Rome, Domus Aurea, Boudicca's uprising, and Jewish revolt belong here. His death in 68 CE ends the dynasty and leads to civil war.

## Key connections

- Tiberius' praetorian prefect who fell in 31 CE was **Sejanus**. Tiberius spent his later years on Capri.

- The emperor whose nickname meant little boot was **Caligula**. He was a son of Germanicus and Agrippina the Elder.

- The officer associated with Caligula's assassination was Cassius **Chaerea**. Claudius succeeded Caligula in 41 CE.

- The general who led Claudius' invasion of Britain was Aulus **Plautius**. The campaign began the durable Roman conquest of Britain.

- Nero's tutor and early adviser was **Seneca** the Younger. Afranius Burrus was another major early adviser.

- The Iceni queen who revolted under Nero was **Boudicca**. Suetonius Paulinus suppressed the revolt.
