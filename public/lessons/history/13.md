## Overview

Crossing the Rubicon turns the dispute over Caesar's command into civil war. His campaigns move through Spain, Greece, Africa, and back to Spain. Pompey's defeat does not immediately end resistance: other leaders continue the war after his death.

Keep Zela separate from the civil war against Pompey's supporters. After the wars, Caesar accumulates honors and receives a lifetime dictatorship. The conspiracy led by Brutus and Cassius ends his life but fails to restore political stability.

## Key connections

- Caesar crossed the **Rubicon** in 49 BCE. The crossing marked the start of civil war.

- Caesar defeated Pompey at **Pharsalus** in 48 BCE. Pompey fled to Egypt and was killed there.

- At Zela, Caesar defeated **Pharnaces** II. He was Mithridates VI's son; this was a separate eastern campaign.

- After Thapsus, Cato the Younger died at **Utica**. This explains his name Cato Uticensis.

- Caesar's final Spanish victory was at **Munda** in 45 BCE. His opponents included Pompey's sons and Titus Labienus.

- Caesar was assassinated on March 15 in **44** BCE. Marcus Junius Brutus and Gaius Cassius Longinus led the conspiracy.
