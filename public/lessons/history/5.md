## Overview

Consuls replace kings, while conflict between patricians and plebeians shapes internal politics. Early military narratives celebrate citizens who defend Rome and then relinquish extraordinary authority. Cincinnatus is the clearest example of that ideal.

Build a chain from the first plebeian secession to written law and then to changes in marriage and officeholding. The Twelve Tables are a written legal code; the Lex Canuleia is a particular later law. The guide's traditional dates provide anchors for this sequence.

## Key connections

- At Lake Regillus, tradition says Rome was helped by Castor and **Pollux**. The Dioscuri are associated with the battle of 496 BCE.

- The first secession of the plebs occurred in **494** BCE. This reflects plebeian pressure within the new Republic.

- The dictator who rescued a Roman army at Mount Algidus in 458 BCE was **Cincinnatus**. He returned to his farm after the emergency.

- Rome's early written legal code was the Twelve **Tables**. The decemvirs compiled it in 451-450 BCE.

- The law permitting patrician-plebeian intermarriage was the Lex **Canuleia**. The guide dates it to 445 BCE.

- The first plebeian consul in 366 BCE was Lucius Sextius **Lateranus**. Link him with the Licinian-Sextian legislation of 367 BCE.
