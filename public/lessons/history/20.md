## Overview

From 235 to 284 CE, emperors rise and fall rapidly through army support and assassination. Goths threaten the Balkans, and Sasanian Persia challenges Rome in the east. Learn a few anchors before adding the shorter reigns from the guide's emperor list.

The Empire also fragments. Postumus establishes a Gallic regime; Zenobia expands Palmyrene power in the east. Aurelian restores unity. The later sequence leads to Diocletian and a new system of government.

## Key connections

- The soldier-emperor after Severus Alexander was Maximinus **Thrax**. His reign began in 235 CE.

- The emperor who celebrated Rome's thousandth anniversary was Philip the **Arab**. His reign was 244-249 CE.

- The emperor killed by the Goths at Abrittus was **Decius**. He died in 251 CE.

- The emperor captured by Shapur I was **Valerian**. His capture was a major disaster in the Persian war.

- The founder of the separate Gallic Empire was **Postumus**. His breakaway regime began in 260 CE.

- The emperor who defeated Zenobia and reunited the Empire was **Aurelian**. His title Restitutor Orbis means restorer of the world.
