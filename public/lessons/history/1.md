## Overview

Start with a timeline, then attach people, places, and causes to it. Harrison organizes the narrative into monarchy, Republic, and Empire, with supporting lists of emperors and monuments. These lessons follow that sequence and summarize selected facts in fresh language. They provide a foundation for reading the full guide, including its more advanced details.

Treat the stories about Rome's kings as Roman tradition, rather than contemporary eyewitness records. For later events, learn a battle together with its commanders and consequence. BCE and CE correspond to the guide's B.C. and A.D. Read a lesson, answer its recall questions, and return to due cards on later days.

## Key connections

- The traditional founder of Rome is **Romulus**. Begin the monarchy timeline with the foundation stories.

- Rome was traditionally founded in **753** BCE. Pair the year with Romulus and the Palatine Hill.

- The Republic traditionally begins in **509** BCE. This follows the expulsion of the last king.

- The grant of the name Augustus in **27** BCE conventionally begins the Empire. Octavian becomes Augustus.

- Romulus Augustulus was deposed in **476** CE. This is the conventional endpoint of the Western Empire.

- Harrison organizes the main narrative into monarchy, Republic, and **Empire**. Use these periods as a framework for detailed study.
